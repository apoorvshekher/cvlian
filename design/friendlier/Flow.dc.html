The page redraws
Two seconds after the last keystroke — long enough that a sentence gets finished before anything happens, and few enough compiles to keep a laptop quiet.
The section you are in is tinted, so you can see where the words landed. The old page holds still until the new one is ready: no flicker, no spinner, no scroll jump.

3 Keeping it Four different meanings of "saved", and only two of them are the person's job.
This browser
Automatic. Every change, a third of a second later, and once more on the way out of the tab.
Clearing site data is the one thing that erases it — which is what the export exists for.
Your Drive
Automatic, if you chose it. A few seconds behind the local save, so the editor never waits on a network.
The chip in the bar says which of the five states it is in, in words: not connected, connecting, synced, syncing, or a problem.
Snapshots Copies
On purpose. A named point you can come back to — "before the big rewrite" — while you carry on editing.
Restore it over what you have, or open it as a copy of its own.
Copies
On purpose. One per job worth tailoring for — "Data Scientist", "Frontend". Each carries its own snapshots.
The same feature that is called a branch today, under the word people already use — renamed in the interface only. The exported JSON keeps saying branches, so every backup ever written still imports.
Two machines that both moved on: the newer working copy stays live and the older one is kept as a snapshot inside the same copy — nothing is discarded and no merge screen ever appears. The panel says what it did, in one line, and you can restore the other side. This rule already exists in the app; the flow just has to say it out loud.
4 When it goes wrong Four real failures. In none of them does the page go blank or the writing stop.
A stray character in a bullet Trouble
By far the most common failure, and the app already knows which line it was. A sentence, not a log. The bullet is quoted with the offending character marked, and one button removes it. The last good page stays on screen with a note saying how old it is.
The typesetter didn't load
The WASM engine or its format file failed to arrive — a bad network, or a half-deployed site. One line and a Retry, where the page would be. Writing and saving carry on working; only the PDF is unavailable, and the bar says so.
Drive said no
The access token is in memory only, so it expires — closing the laptop is enough. The chip turns amber and says "needs a reconnect". One click re-consents, silently if Google will allow it. Every edit in the meantime is already saved locally and pushes on reconnect.
Offline
The whole app is static and the typesetter's files are cached, so the loop in band 2 keeps running. Nothing is announced beyond the Drive chip saying it is waiting. The push goes out when the network comes back. No queue for the person to manage.
5 Leaving with something The point of the whole thing, and the one place a button is the right answer.
Download the PDF
The one primary button in the bar. Named from the resume and the copy — Alex_Doe_Resume_Data_Scientist.pdf — so a folder of applications stays legible.
Open it in a tab
For reading at full size, printing, or dropping into an application form. The preview keeps its own small link for this.
Export a backup
One JSON file with every copy and every snapshot. The answer to "what if I clear my browser", and the way into another machine.
Just close the tab
Already saved, both places. Coming back skips band 1 entirely and lands on the copy that was open.
